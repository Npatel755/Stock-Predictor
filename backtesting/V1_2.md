## Rules changed

The original completed-daily/4H bias, confirmed pivots, 15m breakout, separate retest/confirmation, daily lock, and $100 planned risk remain. This candidate changes:

1. Reward/risk target: **4:1**, previously 2:1.
2. Stop buffer beyond the retest extreme: **0.5 ATR**, previously 0.1 ATR.
3. Entry volatility filter: five-minute ATR must be at least **10 basis points (0.10%)** of price.
4. Size includes estimated round-trip commission and slippage in the $100 planned risk denominator.

This seeks higher net expectancy rather than a higher win rate. In the full historical research replay the win rate is 29.3%, compared with 39.9% for the original cost-scenario baseline. Increasing risk or leverage was not counted as improvement.

## First experiment results

256 predefined candidates were screened on 2023–2024. The training shortlist was evaluated on two halves of 2025; the choice was frozen before inspecting its 2026 retrospective audit. All periods in this dataset had been inspected previously in the project, so none is advertised as a fresh holdout.

Over January 1, 2023 through October 27, 2026, under the same Python execution/cost assumptions:

| Measure | Unchanged v1.1 cost scenario | v1.2 candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Net P&L | −$652.09 | +$3,638.52 |
| Return on $50,000 | −1.30% | +7.28% |
| Entries | 168 | 147 |
| Profit factor | 0.948 | 1.348 |
| Drawdown from running unrealized peak | 4.11% | 2.80% |

The candidate made **−$96.11** in 2025 H1 and **+$1,310.75** in H2. It therefore **failed** the predefined requirement that both validation halves be profitable. This failure remains recorded even though aggregate P&L improved. v1.2 is experimental and is not promoted to a verified funded-account strategy.

The 2025 slippage sensitivity remains profitable at 8bp extra assumed slippage per market/stop side (+$791.19), versus 2bp in the main comparison (+$1,214.64). This is a historical sensitivity result, not a future-profit prediction. The 2026 paired uncertainty interval for improvement includes zero. Nearby parameter settings and all failed candidates are retained in experiment artifacts.

## Run in Python

From the repository root, using the existing environment:

```powershell
.ethvenv/Scripts/python.exe -m backtesting run --mode research --config backtesting/configs/dvs_eth_v1_2.json --end 2026-10-28 --output backtesting/artifacts/dvs/v1_2
```

In the dashboard, choose **B · Research → Research preset → DVS ETH v1.2 (experimental)**. The preset loads its fixed rules, displays them, and selects a complete date range. Manual risk inputs are disabled while the preset is selected. The original parity mode remains available separately.

The JSON's `strategy_version: v1.1` identifies the supplied base state machine. The profile and exported derivative are named **v1.2**, with the extra research fields providing the changed rules.

## Execution limits

Python assumes next-open entries, 4bp commission per side, two ticks plus 2bp adverse slippage on market/stop orders, 3.3bp/day financing, and pessimistic stop-first ambiguity handling. The Pine derivative implements the experimental signal/risk rules and next-open entries, but TradingView's fixed-tick slippage does not implement the additional basis-point fill slippage, financing, or the pessimistic OHLC policy. These differences appear in its file header. It has not been compiled or reconciled in TradingView yet; Python exporter tests do not prove Pine compilation.

No live execution has been added. The unavailable October 28 and November 2 candles have not been fabricated. Future paper observations and actual execution-feed reconciliation are still needed.

Further research is continuing in `round2_v1_2_neighborhood`. That search is explicitly disclosed as reusing 2025 selection data. It does not silently overwrite the saved v1.2 file.
