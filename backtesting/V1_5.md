## Rules

Use standard **BINANCE:ETHUSDT.P, five-minute candles**. Signals use completed hourly candles and need at least 150 hours for warm-up.

1. Buy when the hourly close exceeds the preceding 96 hours' highs and is above hourly EMA 150. Sell short below the preceding 96 hours' lows while below that EMA.
2. Require completed-hour volume to be at least **2 times the mean volume of the preceding 20 completed hours**. The current hour is excluded from that denominator.
3. Enter at the next five-minute open after the hour closes. One position at a time; both directions and UTC weekends allowed.
4. Initial stop: 1.5 hourly ATR(14) from the signal close. Target: 4.5 times the initial stop distance. The bracket is not silently rebased to a future fill.
5. Tighten a 3 hourly ATR trailing stop from each completed five-minute close using only the latest completed hourly ATR. Stop changes affect subsequent ticks.
6. Keep $100 planned risk including estimated costs, maximum 5x notional leverage, three entries per UTC day and a $300 personal daily entry gate. These are personal strategy controls, not funded-account compliance certification.

## More data: what was added and what it showed

The v1.5 rules and comparators were frozen before requesting additional data. The original datasets and the numbered profiles were protected.

Native Binance ETH perpetual archives for November/December 2019 were unavailable. Missing October 28 and November 2, 2026 daily archives remained unavailable, and Binance's REST endpoint returned HTTP 451 for the requested location. The other requested recent days were already in the old dataset, so they do not count as new information. Massive's configured API returned HTTP 403 for the older ETH/USD date range. No access restriction was bypassed and no gaps were filled with fabricated candles.

Instead, **208,855 checksum-verified five-minute Binance ETHUSDT spot bars from 2018-2019** were fetched from the official public archive. This adds an earlier bear-market regime from a different market. Instrument labels remain `BINANCE:ETHUSDT`; spot records are not passed off as perpetual futures or Breakout execution.

The early source archives included 241 records off the five-minute timestamp grid. They were excluded and counted without shifting their times; original ZIPs remain intact. The resulting dataset has 1,385 missing five-minute intervals. The audit splits at actual gaps and annual boundaries, resets each account and indicator warm-up, and rejects segments shorter than 2,400 candles. Four short segments were skipped. It does not forward-fill prices or manufacture volume.

**v1.5 was profitable but trailed v1.4 on this additional feed.** The filter's historical profit improvement is not universal. These sums are not a continuous-account return; each segment starts a separate $50,000 account. The same hypothetical leveraged futures-style cost scenario is applied for comparison, including financing; it is not a claim about actual spot funding or executable spot shorting. Open positions remain marked at segment endpoints.

The new data was used to evaluate the fixed rules, not select new thresholds. Earlier-regime cross-market transfer does not establish prospective performance. The original comparison's 638 candidate slots, repeated tuning and previously inspected 2026 still create selection bias. More data helps expose weaknesses; it does not remove that history.

Copy the entire text download into a new TradingView Pine Editor strategy on the standard five-minute ETH perpetual chart. The title is **ETH v1.5 | Volume filtered channel research**.

Compilation and exact trade matching remain unverified. Pine implements the volume filter but models only fixed-tick fill slippage. It omits Python's extra basis-point fill slippage, financing and stop-first ambiguity policy. History initialization, quantity/tick precision and broker ordering can also change totals. Do not expect equal headline Python/TradingView results, and do not treat this historical research as funded-trading readiness.
