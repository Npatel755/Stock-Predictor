# Research protocol

The user's current transfer protocol trains ES/NQ on available 2010–2023 reference history. Internal fitting ends before 2022 for chronological reference selection in 2022–2023; selected ML candidates are then refitted using all eligible reference observations through 2023, with calibration still confined to that training period. No ES/NQ observations from 2024 onward enter fitting. Actual MES/MNQ 2024–2025 is external-instrument validation; actual micro 2026 remains final test. The transfer study excludes volume/VWAP features, preserves real contract identities and multipliers, and never fabricates pre-2019 micro P&L. Its morning-only replay uses one account without fold resets and rejects measured drawdown above $2,000. A $1,800 risk stop leaves limited headroom, without guaranteeing a future maximum loss. Model-feature inputs use full causal history; replay can omit flat off-window bars only under tested accounting equivalence.

The question remains unanswered on real market data. Software tests and synthetic P&L do not establish an investment edge.

The active Databento catalog has physical chronological partitions fixed before fitting: available history through 2023 for training, 2024–2025 for validation and 2026 through November 5 for test, using UTC calendar boundaries. MES/MNQ begin in May 2019; ES/NQ begin in June 2010 and remain separate mini-futures references. No rows are randomly shuffled between periods. The split protocol, source hashes and exact counts are recorded in `reports/DATA_SPLIT_REPORT.md` and partition manifests.

Default `validation_mode: fixed` fits only training-period rows, limited to the most recent `maximum_training_rows` when necessary for resources. The same fitted model is scored across chronological validation blocks. Optional `walk_forward` mode may use completed earlier validation observations for later fits and must be disclosed; it never accesses the final test. Standalone fixture imports retain their configured final-fraction protocol. Training events are purged when their label end reaches a validation boundary, with a configurable embargo in expanding folds. Labels start at the next executable bar open and cannot cross a roll or extended gap. Calibration uses a later subsection of training, with its own purge; scaler, imputer, variance filtering and correlation pruning fit only earlier eligible observations.

The baseline campaign runs 16 traditional candidates before ML and applies identical contract size, cost, stop/target, latency and risk settings. Models may abstain. Ridge predicts forward returns; classifiers predict direction. Cost-aware three-way labels and triple-barrier/meta-label utilities are available for separately registered objective comparisons. The default campaign uses forward-return direction; multiple objectives, horizons, feature sets and hyperparameters are separate experiments, not silently pooled into one result.

`optimization.nested_search` supports a bounded chronological inner grid or optional Optuna study. Its inputs must be restricted to an outer training partition. Default campaign parameters are predeclared and are not tuned using outer validation. Probability calibration supports sigmoid and isotonic; OOS Brier, log loss and reliability bins are recorded. Regime thresholds are fixed causal functions; optional K-means/GMM fit only eligible training history. Performance weighting must use inner training OOS returns; default ensemble weights are equal.

Each validation fold starts flat with the same research capital and risk state, and ends with an explicitly synthetic accounting liquidation. Fold curves are joined additively for attribution. This is **not** a continuous funded-account track record. Funded replay is separately run on the selected chronological OOS signal sequence; all fold resets and truncated intervals must be considered when interpreting it.

The registry records dataset/config/source/model hashes, parent commit, instrument/timeframe, seed, costs, risk, event horizon, parameter attempts, failure reasons and chronological boundaries. Single-thread CPU budgets avoid interference. Missing dependencies and small training event counts remain visible failures. Runtime limits are checked between fits; a single estimator call is not preempted. No search is allowed to exhaust unbounded disk or parallel jobs.

Reported uncertainty uses circular session block bootstrap with weak stationarity and finite sample assumptions. A centered maximum-mean block test across supplied strategies provides a Reality Check-related diagnostic. A separate IID probabilistic/deflated Sharpe diagnostic discloses its weaker serial-independence assumptions and uncertain effective trial count. Bonferroni-adjusted bootstrap p-values are conservative diagnostics, not proof that every prior research choice has been accounted for. Short samples are flagged. Calendar-year/regime attribution does not create independent observations.

After selection, freeze candidate definition, artifact hash, code commit, source hash, dataset version, config hash and final-test boundary. `holdout` exclusively creates a global dataset/boundary reservation before evaluating; any failed run consumes it. Repeated access is rejected. Source or artifact changes invalidate the frozen candidate. The owner must preserve these files; local filesystem guards do not defend against deliberate deletion or moving the governance store. New research needs a new forward period or an explicitly disclosed protocol reset.

Eligibility needs positive after-cost OOS expectancy with a positive bootstrap interval, adequate trades and sessions, fold stability, cost robustness, verified raw prices/calendar, and account compliance. Actual deployment is always disabled. A no-trade model may outrank losing models in a functional test but is not a viable trading candidate.
